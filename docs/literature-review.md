# 3. Literature Review

## 3.1 Critical Review

Existing research relevant to our banking analytics web service covers PDF extraction, transaction graphs, personal finance visualization, financial question answering, and data security.

For PDF processing, Mikhailov et al. (2021) and Potvin et al. (2016) describe text- and position-based extraction methods suitable for supported text-layer statements. Smock et al. (2022) and Xu et al. (2021) offer more complex table and document understanding approaches, but these may be unnecessary for the initial MVP.

Saxena et al. (2021) show how bank transfers can be modeled as directed, weighted graphs, while Oliveira et al. (2023) explore financial spending patterns and visualization. Lewis and Perry (2019) emphasize designing personal finance tools around real users' needs. However, these studies do not directly evaluate multi-bank statements from Kazakhstan.

For the intelligent assistant, FinQA and TAT-QA (Chen et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2021) highlight the importance of accurate financial calculations. BIRD and DIN-SQL (Li et al., 2023; Pourreza & Rafiei, 2023) examine natural-language database queries and their limitations. Edemacu and Wu (2025) and Debenedetti et al. (2024) address privacy and prompt-injection risks. Together, these findings support verified backend calculations, restricted assistant access, and secure handling of personal financial data.

| 7 | Oliveira et al. (2023) | Spending Pattern Visualization Using Unsupervised Machine Learning | Categorized financial transactions, preprocessing, clustering, and visual analysis | Shows how spending profiles can be explored visually with categorized transaction features | Limited dataset and expert-centered assessment; user clustering exceeds MVP scope | Supports transaction categorization, financial summaries, and dashboard design |
| 8 | Chen et al. (2021) | FinQA: A Dataset of Numerical Reasoning over Financial Data | Financial question answering with annotated executable calculation programs | Highlights the importance and difficulty of accurate, explainable financial calculations | Based on corporate financial reports, not private bank transactions | Supports verified arithmetic, aggregation, and explanation of chatbot outputs |
| 9 | Edemacu & Wu (2025) | Privacy Preserving Prompt Engineering: A Survey | Survey of methods protecting private information in prompts and in-context learning | Organizes risks and potential privacy-preserving prompting techniques | Survey is broader than the project's exact architecture; methods still need implementation | Supports data minimization and privacy controls for external LLM requests |

| 13 | Xu et al. ## 3.3 Research Gap and Project Contribution

Existing studies address PDF extraction, financial graphs, visualization, and question answering, but rarely combine these functions for individuals analyzing statements from multiple banks. Their methods are also not validated specifically on Kaspi, Halyk, and Freedom Bank PDFs.

Our project aims to integrate supported text-based statement parsing, transaction normalization, financial summaries, and directed, weighted graphs into one web service. A controlled chat assistant will answer questions using verified backend calculations. The prototype will be evaluated for extraction accuracy, graph correctness, answer reliability, privacy, and usability with potential users.
